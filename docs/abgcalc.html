Structured ABG interpretation at the bedside

Enter pH, PaCO₂, HCO₃⁻, PaO₂, and FiO₂. This tool estimates the primary disorder, checks expected compensation, and can calculate A–a gradient and P/F ratio. Optional labs enable anion gap and delta gap.

Decision-support only. Results depend on assumptions and may not apply in all clinical contexts (e.g., mixed disorders, ventilator changes, chronic CO₂ retention, altitude). Verify clinically.

Advanced oxygenation settings (optional)

Defaults assume sea level and typical RQ. Adjust if needed for your context.

Anion gap & delta gap (optional labs)

Enter sodium and chloride to calculate anion gap: AG = Na − (Cl + HCO₃). Albumin can provide an albumin-corrected AG (rule-of-thumb correction).

Albumin correction (rule-of-thumb): AGcorr ≈ AG + 2.5 × (4.0 − albumin). Delta gap (rule-of-thumb): (AG − 12) / (24 − HCO₃).

Compensation formulas used
  • Metabolic acidosis: Winter’s expected PaCO₂ ≈ 1.5×HCO₃ + 8 (±2).
  • Metabolic alkalosis: expected PaCO₂ ≈ 0.7×(HCO₃−24)+40 (±5).
  • Respiratory acidosis: expected HCO₃ change per +10 PaCO₂: acute +1; chronic +3.5 (rule-of-thumb).
  • Respiratory alkalosis: expected HCO₃ change per −10 PaCO₂: acute −2; chronic −4 (rule-of-thumb).
  • A–a gradient: PAO₂ = FiO₂×(Pb−PH₂O) − (PaCO₂/RQ); A–a = PAO₂ − PaO₂.
  • Expected A–a (age rule-of-thumb): ≈ age/4 + 4 (mmHg).

Bedside notes (non-prescriptive)

• Compensation rules are approximations; acute ventilator changes and chronic respiratory disease can mislead “best fit” logic.
• Consider lactate, ketones, toxidromes, renal function, and clinical trajectory for metabolic processes.
• On high FiO₂, A–a can be large even with modest shunt; interpret with clinical context and ventilation/perfusion factors.
