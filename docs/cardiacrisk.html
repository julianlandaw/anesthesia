Active cardiac condition red flags

Unstable angina / suspected ACS
New/worsening chest pain, ischemic symptoms, or concern for acute coronary syndrome.
Decompensated heart failure
Pulmonary edema, escalating diuretics, new orthopnea/PND, or significant volume overload.
Significant arrhythmia
Symptomatic brady/tachyarrhythmia, high-grade AV block, or poorly controlled rate.
Severe valvular disease symptoms
Syncope/angina/dyspnea with known severe AS, severe MS, etc.
Red-flag definitions

Includes suspected ACS or unstable angina, decompensated heart failure, symptomatic or high-grade arrhythmia, and symptoms associated with known severe valvular disease.

Heart failure
History of HF or pulmonary edema; reduced EF or symptomatic HF.
Cerebrovascular disease
History of stroke or TIA.
Diabetes treated with insulin
Insulin use as a marker for higher-risk diabetes.
Creatinine > 2.0 mg/dL (or significant renal insufficiency)
Use local definitions if different.
RCRI factor definitions

The high-risk-procedure checkbox is the original RCRI criterion; it is not interchangeable with the overall guideline risk category. Ischemic heart disease includes prior MI, angina, positive stress testing, or coronary intervention. Heart failure includes prior HF or pulmonary edema. Cerebrovascular disease includes stroke or TIA. The renal criterion is creatinine >2 mg/dL.

How this tool is intended to be used
  • RCRI is one risk index; it does not capture everything (frailty, pulmonary HTN, severe valvular lesions, etc.).
  • Functional capacity and overall perioperative risk often drive the “do we need more info?” question.
  • Red flags should prompt heightened attention; urgency matters.
  • Use your institution’s pathway for ECG, echo, biomarkers, stress testing, and cardiology input.
Clinical notes

• If the patient has time-sensitive surgery, risk stratification may focus on stabilization/optimization rather than delays.
• Consider baseline ECG for elevated-risk surgery or known cardiac disease per local practice pathways.
• Continue chronic beta-blockers; avoid initiating high-dose beta-blockade immediately pre-op without a plan (institution-dependent).
• Tailor anesthetic plan, hemodynamic goals, and postoperative monitoring based on risk profile and trajectory.

Source: 2024 AHA/ACC guideline for perioperative cardiovascular management
